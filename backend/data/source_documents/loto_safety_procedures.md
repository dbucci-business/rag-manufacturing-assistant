# LOTO & Energy Isolation Safety Procedures

> **CRITICAL SAFETY DOCUMENT:** Non-compliance with LOTO and energy isolation procedures is a terminable offense and may result in serious injury or death. All personnel must be trained and authorized before performing any LOTO.

## 1. Purpose & Scope

This procedure establishes mandatory Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) and energy isolation requirements for all maintenance activities at Vulcan Tire Corporation. It applies to every person performing maintenance, servicing, or repair work on any equipment at VTC facilities. The procedure conforms to OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147 – Control of Hazardous Energy.

In tire manufacturing, hazardous energy sources include: electrical (480V/6,600V), hydraulic (up to 3,000 PSI), pneumatic (up to 150 PSI plant air), steam (up to 250 PSI, 185°C), thermal (hot rubber, hot molds, steam lines), gravitational (suspended loads, elevated platforms), and chemical (process chemicals, lubricants). Every energy source must be identified and isolated before work begins.

## 2. LOTO Roles & Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Authorized Employee | Person performing LOTO and the maintenance work. Applies their personal lock. Must hold current VTC LOTO certification (annual retraining required). |
| Affected Employee | Operators and others who work in the area where LOTO is applied. Must be notified before LOTO is applied and again upon its removal. |
| LOTO Coordinator | Maintenance Supervisor overseeing complex or group LOTO procedures. Responsible for ensuring all energy sources are identified and all locks are removed upon job completion. |

## 3. Standard LOTO Procedure (6-Step Method)

### Step 1 – Prepare for Shutdown

Review the equipment-specific Energy Control Procedure (ECP) from the LOTO binder located at each department LOTO station. Identify all energy sources on the ECP. Gather required locks, tags, and hasps. Notify all affected employees.

### Step 2 – Notify All Affected Employees

Inform all operators and workers in the affected area that the equipment is being shut down for maintenance and LOTO will be applied. Do not begin shutdown until all affected employees acknowledge notification. Upon shutdown, employees not involved in maintenance are to leave the area or not come to work during the duration of the time.

### Step 3 – Shut Down the Equipment

Use the normal stopping procedure for the equipment (HMI E-stop, equipment-specific shutdown sequence). Ensure the equipment has come to a complete stop and all moving parts have ceased motion before proceeding.

### Step 4 – Isolate All Energy Sources

Apply isolation devices at each energy source identified on the ECP in the sequence specified. For equipment with multiple energy types, isolation must follow this order:

1. Electrical: Open main disconnect; place in OFF/OPEN position
2. Pneumatic: Close supply isolation valve; bleed down to 0 PSI
3. Hydraulic: Close HPU isolation valve; release stored pressure via bleed port
4. Steam: Close steam supply and condensate valves; allow to cool
5. Gravitational: Block or secure any suspended components

### Step 5 – Apply LOTO Devices

Apply your personal lock and tag to each isolation point. If multiple maintenance technicians are working, each person applies their own lock using a hasp. The LOTO tag must include: name, date, contact number, and work order number. Never share locks.

### Step 6 – Verify Zero Energy State (TRIFECTA CHECK)

Perform the Trifecta Energy Verification before touching the equipment:

- **CHECK:** Attempt to restart equipment from operator control panel (expect no response)
- **TEST:** Test for voltage at exposed terminals using a calibrated CAT III or higher meter
- **VERIFY:** Visually confirm all isolation points are in the isolated position and locked

## 5. Group LOTO Procedures

When multiple maintenance technicians, contractors, or departments are working on the same equipment simultaneously (e.g., annual Banbury overhaul), a Group LOTO procedure must be used. A group LOTO hasp must accommodate all individual locks. The LOTO Coordinator applies a master lock and is responsible for personnel accountability.

A Group LOTO log sheet must be maintained listing each authorized employee who has applied a lock. No lock may be removed except by the person who applied it. The LOTO Coordinator is the last to remove the master lock after confirming all individual locks are removed and all personnel are clear.

## 6. LOTO Training Requirements

All authorized employees must complete initial LOTO training and an annual refresher. Training must be documented in the VTC Training Tracking System and is verified during the eMaint technician qualification check before PM assignment. Retraining is required immediately after any LOTO incident, near-miss, or violation.

## 7. Confined Space Entry – Integration with LOTO

Several tire manufacturing assets require confined space entry during maintenance (Banbury mixer chambers, underground utility tunnels). Confined space entry must be preceded by a LOTO that fully isolates the space plus a Confined Space Entry Permit (VTC-SAF-CSE-012). Both procedures run concurrently and neither may be removed while work is in progress.
